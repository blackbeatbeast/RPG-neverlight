# 02 — Product vision

## Fantasy

You are a licensed “Pathfinder” in a city built around dormant relic infrastructure. Each click opens a tiny page of the world: a rain-dark alley, an overgrown signal tower, a bounty notice, an appraiser’s half-smile, a monster described in two sharp lines. You choose one to three actions, receive a compact battle report, and inspect whatever strange object survived.

## Pillars in player language

### “I can play in a minute”
A useful micro-session fits into one phone unlock: collect a dispatch, explore once, resolve a turn queue, inspect/salvage loot, leave.

### “The page itself feels like an artifact”
The interface evokes an old personal web page or feature-phone portal through borders, small illustrations, numbered links, stamps, compact meters, and deliberate typography—not through broken responsiveness or illegible text.

### “Every rare thing has a story”
Rare equipment and Memory Cards carry provenance, discovery date, source, affix history, and codex context. Value comes from build identity and history, not merely a bigger number.

### “Other players are present without demanding my attention”
Players leave traces, discoveries, wanted notices, market listings, guild messages, and rescue echoes. Social presence is asynchronous and blockable.

### “The women are charming adults, not interchangeable rewards”
Adult characters have jobs, motives, flaws, relationships, mechanical roles, and evolving dialogue. Suggestiveness can exist as costume/tone, never as age ambiguity or explicit sexual content.

## Original setting seed

The city of **Vesper Ark** survives around a buried network called the **Lattice**. Relic devices occasionally “remember” impossible people, places, and techniques. Pathfinders recover these fragments before storms rewrite the routes.

This setting seed is provisional. Names require clearance before public use.

## Session shapes

- **60 seconds:** claim dispatch, run one safe route, salvage duplicates.
- **5 minutes:** explore a chain, queue combat, adjust build, post a finding.
- **20 minutes:** dungeon route, boss preparation, crafting, codex review.
- **Long-tail:** optimize affix interactions, complete card sets, trade later, guild projects, seasonal discoveries.

## Emotional arc

1. Curiosity: “What is behind this link?”
2. Recognition: “I know this kind of old page.”
3. Competence: “I can read the compact combat log.”
4. Attachment: “This item/card/character has history.”
5. Belonging: “Other people are solving the same world.”
6. Mastery: “My build changes what choices are correct.”

## Anti-pillars

- no casino-shaped progression;
- no intrusive daily chore wall;
- no unreadable mobile spreadsheet;
- no fake nostalgia that simply reproduces latency or poor navigation;
- no character art that undermines public distribution, ad eligibility, or player trust;
- no economy where the richest player buys combat dominance.
